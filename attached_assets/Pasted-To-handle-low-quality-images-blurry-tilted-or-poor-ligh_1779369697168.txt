To handle "low quality" images (blurry, tilted, or poor lighting), you must perform a series of standard preprocessing steps before trying to read the bubbles:Preprocessing:Grayscale Conversion: Convert the image to grayscale to simplify calculations.  Blurring: Use Gaussian Blur to reduce high-frequency noise (especially helpful for grainy or low-resolution camera shots).  Thresholding (Otsu’s): Convert to a binary image. Otsu’s thresholding is excellent here because it automatically finds the best "cut-off" point for black/white pixels even when lighting is uneven.  Geometric Correction (The most important step):Contour Detection: Identify the "corner markers" or the outer rectangular boundary of your OMR sheet.  Perspective Transform: Use these corners to perform a "top-down" warp. This effectively "flattens" the sheet, fixing issues where the photo was taken at an angle.  Extraction:Once the sheet is flattened, you know exactly where the bubbles are based on a fixed template coordinate system.2. How to "Train" or Improve AccuracyYou don't necessarily need a massive neural network (like a full YOLO implementation) if your sheet layout is static. Instead, "train" your system to be more robust by:Dynamic Thresholding: Instead of a hard-coded value for "filled bubble" (e.g., "if pixel count > 100"), use a relative threshold. Calculate the average darkness of all bubbles in a row and define the "filled" one as the outlier (the darkest one significantly darker than the empty ones).Data Augmentation (for testing): If you are using a Machine Learning model (like a CNN) to classify bubbles as "filled" vs "empty," you need to train it on "noisy" data.Take clear images of your OMR sheet and programmatically add noise: rotate them, blur them, adjust brightness/contrast randomly, and simulate shadows.Use a tool like Roboflow or standard Python (Albumentations library) to create a diverse dataset of "bad" quality images labeled with the correct ground truth.Template Matching: Store a "master" template. If the sheet is severely warped or low quality, use Feature Matching (ORB or SIFT algorithms in OpenCV) to align the image to the master template before extraction.3. Recommended ToolkitCore Logic: Python + OpenCV.Mathematics/Arrays: NumPy (for pixel matrix calculations).Model Training (if needed): If you go the AI route, use TensorFlow/Keras or PyTorch to build a simple Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) specifically to classify a cropped bubble image as "marked" or "empty."Workflow Manager: Since you are building offline tools, consider a simple Tkinter or PyQt GUI so you can drag and drop images into your folder to process them instantly.
